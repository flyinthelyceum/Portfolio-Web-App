# Responsive Sculpture in Augmented Space

## The Concept

What if a sculpture wasn't a fixed object, but something that morphed based on how you looked at it?

This project uses WebAR to place 3D objects in physical space. But unlike static sculptures, these forms are alive—they respond to the viewer's position, creating a unique experience for each person.

## How It Works

* **Position tracking**: The device knows where the viewer is relative to the object
* **Algorithmic transformation**: The sculpture continuously recalculates its form based on viewer position
* **Smooth interpolation**: Changes happen fluidly, never feeling jarring
* **Layered complexity**: The deeper you explore, the more detail reveals itself

## The Visual Language

The sculptures are built from geometric primitives (spheres, cubes, polyhedra) arranged and morphed in ways that feel almost alive. They have a quality of intelligence—as if they're "aware" of being observed.

## Philosophical Grounding

This work engages with ideas from physics and perception:
* **Relativity**: The object is different depending on where you observe from
* **Quantum mechanics (loosely)**: Observation changes the system
* **Phenomenology**: Emphasizing embodied experience over detached viewing

## Technical Implementation

* **Three.js**: 3D rendering and geometry manipulation
* **AR.js**: Web-based augmented reality
* **GLSL shaders**: Custom vertex shaders for morphing geometry
* **Optimized for mobile**: Runs smoothly on standard smartphones

## The Experience

Visitors move around the sculpture, watching it respond. Initially there's confusion—"why is it changing?" But gradually, the interaction becomes intuitive. You understand the relationship between your position and the form. You become a collaborator in sculpting the space.

## Future Directions

* Multi-user shared space (sculptures respond to multiple viewers simultaneously)
* Time-based variations (sculpture evolves over hours/days)
* Integration with sound and other sensory modalities
