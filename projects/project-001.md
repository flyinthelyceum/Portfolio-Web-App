# Sonic Landscapes

## About This Project

This project investigates how sound can define space. Not as background or accompaniment, but as the primary medium through which visitors experience environment.

## The Installation

A darkened room equipped with spatial audio. Visitors wear headphones and navigate a 3D soundscape using hand gestures tracked in real-time. As they move, the sonic landscape responds.

## The Sound Design

Rather than traditional music, the audio environment is built from:
* **Granular synthesis**: Breaking down recorded sounds into tiny grains and reassembling them
* **Spatial positioning**: Creating the illusion that sounds are located at specific points in 3D space
* **Responsive layering**: More sounds emerge as the visitor explores further into the soundscape

## Why This Matters

In our image-saturated culture, sound is often an afterthought. This project argues that sound deserves primacy. Sound creates intimacy—it enters you directly.

## Technical Challenges

* **Head tracking**: Mapping listener position to sound source location
* **Latency**: Audio must respond immediately to maintain immersion
* **Spatial rendering**: Implementing HRTF (head-related transfer function) processing for believable 3D audio

## Future Iterations

* Collaborative soundscapes with multiple visitors
* Physical objects that "sing" when touched
* Permanent installation exploring seasonal changes in sound
