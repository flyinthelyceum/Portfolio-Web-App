# INITIAL_ROADMAP.md — Context for Portfolio App Alignment

## What This Document Is

This document explains how the **Initial Roadmap** is used to establish course architecture across studio-based classes, and how the portfolio web app is intentionally designed to align with that architecture.

The portfolio app is not a standalone product.  
It is an infrastructural layer inside a larger instructional system.

Understanding this context is essential for correct onboarding language, feature decisions, and UX posture.

---

## What the Initial Roadmap Is

The **Initial Roadmap** is a one-question-at-a-time conversation framework used at the start of each course build or revision.

Its purpose is to:
- surface the *reality* of how a course already functions
- define a shared identity and posture
- establish durable constraints for teaching, critique, and workflow
- generate a coherent set of course documents from a single source of truth

The roadmap produces **course architecture**, not lesson plans.

---

## What the Initial Roadmap Produces

Each run of the Initial Roadmap generates or informs the following artifacts:

- `COURSE_STYLE_GUIDE.md`  
  The canonical spine of the course: voice, tone, media grammar, critique posture, and values.

- `STUDIO_OPERATIONS.md`  
  How studio time actually functions day to day, including expectations around making, critique, revision, and documentation.

- `ASSIGNMENT_TEMPLATE.md`  
  The structural grammar for assignments, independent of specific content.

- `CRITIQUE_PROTOCOL.md`  
  How feedback is given, received, and translated into revision.

- `REVISION_GUIDE.md`  
  A shared definition of what revision is and why it matters.

- `REFERENCE_LIBRARY.md`  
  A curated, evolving set of references that orient taste and seriousness.

- `COURSE_REVISION_QUICK_CHECK.md`  
  A one-page instructor-facing guardrail against drift.

Together, these documents define **how creative practice is taught and sustained**, not just what students make.

## Where Documentation and Portfolio Practice Fit

Within the roadmap, **documentation is defined as a daily studio practice**, not an administrative task.

Documentation is treated as:
- a witness to creative practice
- evidence of revision and growth
- material for critique and reflection
- the foundation of a living portfolio

Crucially:
> A portfolio is not a highlight reel.  
> It is a record of sustained attention.

The portfolio web app exists to **support this practice**, not to invent it.

---

## What the Portfolio App Is (and Is Not)

### The portfolio app *is*:

- A persistent home for documented creative practice
- A place where drafts, iterations, decisions, and reflections can live over time
- A tool that makes revision visible
- A bridge between day-to-day studio work and long-term authorship

### The portfolio app is *not*:

- A submission replacement for the LMS
- A grading interface
- A social feed
- A résumé builder
- A highlight-only gallery

Those functions already exist elsewhere or actively undermine the studio ethos.

---

## How the Portfolio App Aligns with Course Architecture

The portfolio app should reflect the following principles established by the Initial Roadmap:

### 1. Process Over Polish
The app should privilege:
- in-progress work
- versioning
- reflection
- growth over time

Polish is contextual and arrives late.

---

### 2. Documentation as Practice
The app assumes:
- frequent, lightweight documentation
- low friction capture
- imperfect records that accumulate meaning

Documentation should feel natural inside studio rhythm, not like an extra task.

---

### 3. Revision Visibility
The app should make it easy to:
- see change over time
- understand what was revised and why
- connect feedback to action

Revision is a core literacy, and the app should reinforce that.

---

### 4. Authorship, Not Performance
The app should support:
- individual voice
- long-term identity formation
- coherence across projects

It should resist:
- comparison
- ranking
- gamification
- performative metrics

---

## Implications for Onboarding Language

Onboarding should:

- Explain *why* documentation matters before explaining *how*
- Frame the portfolio as a living record, not a finished product
- Normalize imperfection and incompleteness
- Emphasize continuity across projects and semesters
- Make clear that the portfolio grows with the student

Avoid language that suggests:
- “final showcase”
- “best work only”
- “professional branding”
- “content creation”

Those ideas contradict the underlying course architecture.

---

## Summary

The Initial Roadmap establishes the **conditions for creative practice** across a course.

The portfolio web app exists to **support, extend, and stabilize those conditions** over time.

If the app feels aligned, it will:
- feel quiet
- feel serious
- feel humane
- feel durable

If it feels flashy or performative, it is misaligned.

This document should be used as a reference point for:
- onboarding copy
- feature decisions
- UX posture
- long-term roadmap planning
