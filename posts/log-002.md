# Web AR Experiment: Placing Sculptures in Real Space

## Overview

This project investigates the peculiar experience of seeing digital objects in real space through a phone camera. What happens when the virtual has physical presence?

## The Idea

Using WebAR (powered by Three.js and AR.js), visitors point their phone camera at a floor and can place 3D sculptures into their environment. These aren't decorative—they're deliberately awkward, slightly unsettling digital objects. The discomfort is intentional.

Each sculpture is constructed from simple geometric primitives but arranged in ways that feel almost organic. They have a strange character—familiar enough to recognize, uncanny enough to provoke.

## Technical Approach

* **WebAR Framework**: AR.js for marker detection and plane detection
* **3D Rendering**: Three.js for geometry and materials
* **Interaction**: Touch controls to rotate, scale, and position objects
* **Performance**: Optimized for mobile devices with limited resources

The most challenging part was making the AR feel *natural*. Lighting the digital objects to match the real environment was surprisingly complex. Small details—shadows, reflections, material properties—completely change how credible the AR experience feels.

## Reflections

This work sits at the intersection of art and technology that interests me most: not technology for its own sake, but technology as a tool for expanding what art can be.

When you place a digital sculpture in your room and walk around it, seeing it from different angles, the experience of scale shifts. The object becomes *real* in a way that a flat screen never could. That's powerful.
