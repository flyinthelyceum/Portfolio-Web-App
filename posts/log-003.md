# Typography Walks: Collecting Letters in the City

Started a new personal practice this week: walking through the city with the sole intention of observing typography in the environment.

Not the designed typefaces on storefronts (though those are interesting), but the incidental, forgotten, weathered letters. The neon signs that are flickering off. The carved stone that's half-worn away. The handwritten markers on trucks.

## What I'm noticing

The most interesting typography often isn't intentionally designed—it's the byproduct of years of use and neglect. A painted sign on a warehouse becomes abstracted as the paint peels. Neon lights age into different colors. Handwriting on cardboard has more personality than most typefaces.

There's something about collecting these observations that feels like building a personal archive. Each letter tells a story about time, maintenance, change.

## The project evolves

I'm planning to use these photographs as the basis for a new design project. Not copying the letterforms directly, but capturing the *feeling* they convey—that sense of impermanence and history.

This is a good reminder that the most sophisticated design often comes from careful observation of what already exists.
