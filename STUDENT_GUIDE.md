# Student Guide — Art & Technology Portfolio

Welcome! This is **your working portfolio**. It documents your process, experiments, and iteration—not just finished work.

**Goal:** You should be able to publish and update your portfolio entirely from your browser. No code, no Git, no configuration.

---

## Getting Started (5 minutes)

### 1. Create Your Account

**Option A: Google (Fastest)**
1. Go to: [https://flyinthelyceum.github.io/Portfolio-Web-App/](https://flyinthelyceum.github.io/Portfolio-Web-App/)
2. Click **"Create Account"** → **"Sign up with Google"**
3. Select your school Google account
4. Done! (Your profile picture is imported automatically)

**Option B: Email/Password**
1. Go to the app URL
2. Click **"Create Account"** → scroll to form
3. Enter your email
4. Create a password (must include: 8+ characters, letters, numbers, symbols)
5. Confirm password
6. Click **"Create Account"**

### 2. Set Up Your Profile

After signup, you'll see the profile editor:
1. **Display Name**: Your real name (e.g., "Alex Rivera")
2. **Username**: Your portfolio URL handle (e.g., "alexrivera") — use lowercase, no spaces
3. **Bio**: Short artist statement (optional)
4. **Avatar**: Upload a profile picture (optional)
5. Click **"Save Profile"** → you'll be redirected to your editor

### 3. Your Portfolio Is Live!

Your public portfolio URL: `https://.../?user=yourusername`

Share this with anyone—no login required to view.

## Adding Content

### Working Portfolio Logs (Frequent Updates)

Logs are for documenting your process. Add them frequently (weekly, even daily).

**Log Format:** 3 images · 2 sentences · 1 next step

#### How to Add a Log

1. In your editor, click **+ Log** button
2. Fill in:
   - **Title**: Quick description of what you're exploring (e.g., "Testing generative color palettes")
   - **Images**: Upload up to 3 photos/screenshots (drag to reorder)
   - **Body**: Write 2-3 sentences about what you're working on
   - **Next Step**: What will you try next?
3. Click **"Save Log"**

#### What Makes a Good Log

✅ **Good Log:**
- Shows process, not final product
- Includes images (visual documentation)
- 2-3 sentences explaining the thinking
- Clear next step for iteration

❌ **Avoid:**
- Long essays (use Projects for that)
- No images (visual is important)
- Vague descriptions
- Overly polished, finished work

**Example Log:**
```
Title: "Generative colorspace exploration"
Images: [screenshot of code, color output 1, color output 2]
Body: "Experimenting with HSL colorspace to create harmonious palettes. 
       The third iteration creates unexpected warmth in the shadows. 
       This feels closer to what I want."
Next Step: "Test with actual imagery. Push saturation and value contrast further."
```

### Projects (Deep Dives)

Projects showcase finished or near-finished work with artist statements.

#### How to Add a Project

1. In your editor, click **+ Project** button
2. Fill in:
   - **Title**: Project name
   - **Summary**: One-line description
   - **Body**: Full artist statement (what, why, how)
   - **Images**: Up to 3 images (first is hero image, displayed large)
3. Click **"Save Project"**

#### What Makes a Good Project Statement

- **What:** What did you make?
- **Why:** Why does it matter to you? What were you exploring?
- **How:** What's your process? Tools, techniques, inspiration?
- **So what:** Why should someone care?

**Example Project:**
```
Title: "Color as Memory"
Summary: "Interactive installation exploring how color triggers nostalgia"
Body: "I was interested in how specific colors can evoke emotional memories.
       Using code and light, I created an interactive piece where visitors
       select colors and see images/text from their own memories appear.
       This explores the gap between objective color and subjective memory.
       Made with p5.js, Express.js, and custom hardware."
```

## Best Practices

### Documentation Over Perfection

- Frequent, honest updates beat polished, rare ones
- Show the messy middle (that's where learning happens)
- Include failed experiments (they're valuable)
- Update weekly, even if "unfinished"

### Visual is Everything

- Always include images (at least 1)
- Prefer screenshots/photos to text-only updates
- Good images make your portfolio shareable
- Quality matters more than quantity

### Be Specific

- "Exploring color harmony" → better: "Testing warm-cool contrast in landscape photos"
- Generic titles get skipped → specific titles invite engagement
- Explain your thinking (not just what you did)

### Keep It Public

- Your portfolio is meant to be shared
- Friends, family, teachers, colleges will see it
- Make it something you're proud of

## Troubleshooting

### Forgot Your Password?

1. Go to login page
2. Click **"Forgot password?"**
3. Enter your email
4. Check inbox (check spam folder too)
5. Click reset link in email
6. Create new password

### Google Sign-In Not Working?

- Check that popups are allowed in your browser
- Try clearing cookies
- Try incognito window
- Fallback: Use email/password method instead

### Image Not Uploading?

- Check file size (must be less than 5MB)
- Check format (JPEG or PNG only)
- Check internet connection (slow uploads may timeout)
- Try a different image file

### Can't See My Changes?

- Hard refresh browser (Cmd+Shift+R on Mac, Ctrl+Shift+R on Windows)
- Check that you clicked "Save"
- Check internet connection

### Portfolio URL Not Working?

- Click **"Share Portfolio"** to get current link
- Make sure username doesn't have spaces
- Try the userId version (ask your instructor)
- Check that you clicked "Save Profile" after editing username
